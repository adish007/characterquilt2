## How it works

The entire service is in [relay/core.py](/Users/jaina/Downloads/02-durable-run-relay/relay/core.py:31):

1. `submit()` inserts a SQLite deployment row.
2. `run_once()` marks it `running`.
3. Each asset is written to the JSON-backed fake HubSpot.
4. The provider object is read back.
5. A receipt is stored and the deployment becomes `done`.
6. `recover()` reruns every deployment left `running`.

The provider applies its own rules, notably trimming display names to 40 characters. Its JSON file represents a remote system and, per the assignment, should not itself be repaired.

## The defensible promise

A narrow, supportable contract would be:

- One idempotency key identifies one immutable approved request.
- Resubmitting the same key and payload returns the same deployment; changing the payload raises a conflict.
- A deployment succeeds only after exactly one provider draft per approved asset is read back and matches the approved identity and content.
- “Check again” performs fresh provider reads rather than replaying the receipt.
- Workers cannot concurrently execute the same deployment, and provider access is coordinated around the unsafe fake client.
- Cancellation prevents subsequent writes once observed, but may report already-created partial drafts.
- A failed deployment cannot stop recovery of unrelated deployments.

Even after repair, this design cannot honestly promise atomic all-or-nothing deployment: SQLite and HubSpot do not share a transaction, and the supplied provider has no delete operation. It also cannot prevent later external deletion or mutation—only detect it through a live audit.
