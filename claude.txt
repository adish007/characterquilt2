❯ Understand this repo. Here is some context on what we are doing:              
                                                                                
  # Working context — how we approach these tasks                               
                                                                                
  Hand this to whichever agent picks up the next task. It is method, not        
  domain: it                                                                    
  describes how the previous task was won, what the evaluator actually rewards, 
  and                                                                           
  the specific ways of being wrong that caught us out.                          
                                                                                
  ---                                                                           
                                                                                
  ## 1. Establishing facts: measure, never reason                               
                                                                                
  This is the core habit. **Do not reason about what the code does — run it.**  
                                                                                
  Almost every real finding last time came from executing something, not        
  reading:                                                                      
                                                                                
  - Sweeping a parameter the demo held fixed (page size) revealed the same      
  input                                                                         
    producing three different answers.                                          
  - Running every fixture through every alternative implementation revealed all 
  five                                                                          
    broke the reader — two silently, three by hanging.                          
  - Reconstructing a logged production run by replaying the code against a      
  subset                                                                        
    reproduced its exact numbers.                                               
                                                                                
  **When you state a number, show the command that produced it.** When you      
  write a                                                                       
  plan, put measured "current" and expected "after" values on every item. It    
  makes                                                                         
  the plan checkable and it catches your own errors — three of our plan's items 
  were                                                                          
  wrong in ways only arithmetic exposed.                                        
                                                                                
  ### Test your own conclusions, especially the satisfying ones                 
                                                                                
  The most valuable thing we did was **attack our own strongest claim**. We had 
  argued a reconstruction was confirmed because three numbers matched. On       
  inspection:                                                                   
                                                                                
  - one of the three was algebraically derived from another (`836 = 209 × 4`) — 
  not                                                                           
    independent evidence at all;                                                
  - another constrained the *count* of rows but said nothing about *which*      
  rows;                                                                         
  - and a 15-line script showed the remaining match occurred in **47% of random 
    perturbations**. It was the single most likely outcome by chance.           
                                                                                
  A claim you like is exactly the one to try to break. If you cannot think of a 
  way                                                                           
  to falsify it, that is a bad sign, not a good one.                            
                                                                                
  ### Distinguish structural evidence from numeric coincidence                  
                                                                                
  After that test, the reconstruction still stood — but on *structural* grounds 
  (clean ID-block boundaries, an independent document agreeing) rather than on  
  the                                                                           
  arithmetic we had been citing. Same conclusion, completely different and much 
  more                                                                          
  honest justification. Know which kind of evidence you actually have.          
                                                                                
  ---                                                                           
                                                                                
  ## 2. How to treat numbers in the starter                                     
                                                                                
  **Assume every number quoted anywhere is wrong until you derive it.** Last    
  time the                                                                      
  README said 217, the event log claimed 209, the demo printed 214, and the     
  file had                                                                      
  223 — four numbers, no two describing the same thing, none of them the        
  answer.                                                                       
                                                                                
  - **Check units before comparing.** 217 was *rows*; 209 was *companies*. They 
  were                                                                          
    never comparable, and the apparent contradiction dissolved once that was    
  named.                                                                        
    Watch for this whenever two counts "disagree".                              
  - **A number matching your expectation is not confirmation.** In our case the 
    system reported the right total *for entirely the wrong reasons*: six       
  spurious                                                                      
    extras cancelled six missing items exactly. The shipped check agreed with   
  it.                                                                           
    This is why a count can never be the completeness test — only per-item      
    reconciliation catches it.                                                  
  - **Never hardcode a fixture count**, in code or tests. Compute it            
  (`len(rows)`),                                                                
    or assert an invariant instead.                                             
                                                                                
  ---                                                                           
                                                                                
  ## 3. How to decide things                                                    
                                                                                
  ### Cause versus symptom, stated honestly                                     
                                                                                
  The brief warns: *"Removing a symptom is not the same as removing its cause;  
  both                                                                          
  can be right under time pressure, but shipping one while describing it as the 
  other is not."* Keep an explicit table. Ours had a row that read, in effect,  
  "we                                                                           
  detect this; we cannot prevent it, because the cause is upstream of this      
  repo."                                                                        
  That honesty is worth more than an inflated claim.                            
                                                                                
  ### Asymmetric failure modes decide close calls                               
                                                                                
  When two options are both defensible, ask **which way of being wrong is       
  recoverable**. Ours: merging two records that should stay separate silently   
  deletes one — invisible, and the exact defect we were fixing. Not merging     
  when we                                                                       
  should have produces a visible duplicate — obvious and cheap to undo. That    
  asymmetry, not confidence about the truth, settled it.                        
                                                                                
  ### Report, but admit you decided                                             
                                                                                
  We initially wrote "we report them, we don't decide." That was wrong and the  
  human                                                                         
  caught it: shipping output *requires* deciding. The honest formulation is:    
                                                                                
  > We decided. We chose the direction that fails recoverably, we changed       
  nothing                                                                       
  > irreversible, and we made the decision visible so it can be overruled.      
                                                                                
  "I didn't decide" is a dodge a reviewer will poke at. Never claim it.         
                                                                                
  ### Don't repair what you can't tie to the complaint                          
                                                                                
  There will be **planted noise** — plausible-looking failures with no          
  connection to                                                                 
  the stated problem. Ours was a retryable timeout plus a self-healing cache    
  warning.                                                                      
  Identifying them as noise and *saying so* in the write-up is worth more than  
  fixing them. Second task warns some of the eleven labelled cases are exactly  
  this.                                                                         
                                                                                
  ---                                                                           
                                                                                
  ## 4. How to test                                                             
                                                                                
  Green tests prove nothing on their own. The brief says so: *"`make demo`,     
  `make                                                                         
  test` and `make verify` already run clean on the starter, so a green run is   
  not                                                                           
  evidence that you are done — and neither is a green run of checks you wrote   
  yourself."*                                                                   
                                                                                
  What actually earns confidence:                                               
                                                                                
  - **Adversarial tests.** Build a *correct* artifact, damage it one way at a   
  time,                                                                         
    and require rejection each time. We ran eight — dropped item, doubled       
  output,                                                                       
    orphan record, wrong attribution, unauthorised config, emptied disclosure   
  block,                                                                        
    and a plan that *asserted its own success while missing data*. That last    
  one is                                                                        
    the important one: it proves the check isn't reading the subject's own      
  claim.                                                                        
  - **Mutation testing.** Copy the source to a scratch dir, revert each fix in  
  turn,                                                                         
    confirm a test fails. Seven of seven caught means something; "26 tests      
  pass"                                                                         
    alone means very little.                                                    
    - **Watch for ineffective mutations.** One of mine "survived" — but only    
  because                                                                       
      my replace string didn't match the real line, so nothing was mutated.     
  Always                                                                        
      assert the mutation actually applied before trusting a survivor.          
  - **Tests that assert a limit.** Where something is genuinely undetectable,   
  write                                                                         
    a test asserting the indistinguishability. Then the limit is recorded       
  rather                                                                        
    than assumed, and it can't silently regress into a false claim.             
  - **A guard that stays silent is fine — say so.** We shipped a check that     
  reports                                                                       
    zero on both fixtures. That is the point: *a guard that never fires on the  
  data                                                                          
    it was written against cannot have been fitted to it.* Log it as            
  deliberately                                                                  
    empty so silence isn't read as an omission.                                 
  - **No fixture-specific values in assertions.** No literal IDs, names, or     
  counts.                                                                       
    Build small synthetic inputs for specific behaviours; parameterise over     
  both                                                                          
    fixtures for invariants.                                                    
                                                                                
  ### Know what your held-out set cannot test                                   
                                                                                
  A second fixture is there to distinguish a fix from a fit — but be precise    
  about                                                                         
  its blind spots. Ours could not test the central identity policy (it returned 
  the                                                                           
  same answer under either rule) and could not exercise one whole failure mode. 
  Say                                                                           
  this out loud in the write-up. It reads as rigour, not weakness.              
                                                                                
  ---                                                                           
                                                                                
  ## 5. Reviewing agent work — including your own                               
                                                                                
  **After the agent's work was committed and its own tests were green, review   
  found                                                                         
  seven further defects in it.** Two were in code the agent had already         
  reviewed and                                                                  
  endorsed. Earlier, review of the plan caught four more, one of which would    
  have                                                                          
  broken on the real data.                                                      
                                                                                
  So:                                                                           
                                                                                
  - Do not accept a subagent's report at face value. Re-run its verification    
    yourself, and check the things it wasn't asked about.                       
  - When a reviewer sends findings, **verify each one empirically before        
  agreeing or                                                                   
    disagreeing.** Some will already be fixed; some will be worse than stated.  
  Ours:                                                                         
    one reported issue turned out to break seven records in the actual fixture, 
  not                                                                           
    the hypothetical one or two the report implied.                             
  - Push back where you have evidence, concede fast where you don't. Both were  
    needed last time and both were noticed.                                     
                                                                                
  ---                                                                           
                                                                                
  ## 6. Commits                                                                 
                                                                                
  - One commit per coherent group of changes. Ours was one per letter-group of  
  the                                                                           
    fix plan (A–F), each independently green.                                   
  - **Put the numbers in the message.** Before/after values, the adversarial    
  results,                                                                      
    the mutation results. The commit log becomes evidence, and reviewers read   
  it.                                                                           
  - Never commit straight to a shared branch without being asked. Here the      
  human's                                                                       
    own history was on `main`, so `main` was correct — follow the repo's        
  existing                                                                      
    pattern rather than a default.

  ## 8. Tone in the write-up                                                    
                                                                                
  Plain and specific. State what is guaranteed and what is merely more likely,  
  and                                                                           
  keep those columns honest — "the count is stable" is a guarantee; "the count  
  is                                                                            
  correct" is not, if it rests on a judgment no check can settle. List what you 
  chose not to fix and why. List what stayed open. A submission that names its  
  own                                                                           
  weak points reads far stronger than one that doesn't.

  Grouping c1–c11

  Group: A. Duplicates
  Cases: c2, c4
  Cause: submit() ignores the idempotency key (its own docstring admits it);
  retry() mints a fresh run_id, and external_key = f"{run_id}:{asset_id}" is
  run-scoped — so a retry can never dedupe against the original. Both produce
  8.
  ────────────────────────────────────────
  Group: B. Payload drift
  Cases: c5
  Cause: Same key, payload A then B, both accepted. payload_hash is stored and
  never compared.
  ────────────────────────────────────────
  Group: C. "Verified" is a lie
  Cases: c7, c8
  Cause: "verified": True is a literal in run_once. The readback is fetched and
  never compared to the approved asset. audit() and deployment_summary() count
  the receipt, never the provider — so they cannot detect drift at all (P9).
  c7's "not what we approved" is the 40-char truncation.
  ────────────────────────────────────────
  Group: D. No concurrency control
  Cases: c8
  Cause: Two workers claim one run — there is no claim, lease, or status guard.
  FakeHubSpot._save is whole-file read-modify-write with no lock: lost updates
  and torn reads.
  ────────────────────────────────────────
  Group: E. Cancel not honoured
  Cases: c6
  Cause: run_once never re-reads status; its final UPDATE ... SET status='done'
  overwrites the cancel. recover() only selects 'running', so it isn't recovery
   — it's the in-flight worker finishing.
  ────────────────────────────────────────
  Group: F. Poison run halts the queue
  Cases: c9
  Cause: recover() has no try/except and no ORDER BY. Any
  deterministically-failing row aborts the loop for every row after it —
  matching "run-16 and run-17 never deployed anything". Payload-agnostic: I
  reproduced it with a missing assets key and with duplicate asset_ids.
  ────────────────────────────────────────
  Group: Noise
  Cases: c10, c11
  Cause: c10 is a retryable gateway_timeout whose readback found the object —
  self-healed. c11 is thumbnail_render, a tool this pipeline never calls; it's
  the one TASK.md says is unrelated.

  c3 is the interesting boundary case, not obviously either. Two different
  idempotency keys, identical payload → 8 objects. Nothing in the service can
  distinguish "operator submitted twice by mistake" from "two legitimate
  requests." Deduping on payload hash would silently swallow a real second
  request — the unrecoverable direction. My inclination is to detect and surface
  it, not fix it, but that's a claim worth arguing about in the roadmap.

  Two things worth flagging

  - The demo's own comment is misleading. demo.py:40 says "The destination 
  dropped two objects" — the demo deletes them itself, four lines later. The
  claim it attaches to (make stress comes back short) happens to be true, but
  for a completely different reason.
  - The count can't be the completeness test. assets_approved: 4 and
  objects_deployed: 4 agree in the demo while the stored names are wrong, and
  they still agree after the provider loses everything. Per-item reconciliation
  against the provider is the only check that catches this.

  Not yet established

  I have not pinned down what payload_version: "C" in c9 actually is — I showed
  the mechanism is payload-agnostic, which is the honest version of that claim.
  I also haven't yet checked SQLite lock/busy_timeout behaviour under real
  multi-process (not multi-thread) load, or whether retry()'s copied
  payload_hash can go stale.
